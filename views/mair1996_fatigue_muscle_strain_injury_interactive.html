Mair et al. (1996) fatigue and acute muscle strain injury

This study tested whether fatigue makes muscle more vulnerable to strain injury. The main result was consistent across stretch rates: fatigued rabbit EDL muscle failed at roughly the same length as control muscle, but it absorbed less energy before failure, especially with deeper fatigue and slower stretch.

Where the Energy Gap Appeared

The paper reports that most of the difference in energy absorption showed up early in the stretch. That supports the idea that fatigue reduces protective energy handling before the final rupture zone.

Fatigued vs Force-Matched Submaximal Muscle

In the second experiment, a fatigued muscle and a submaximally stimulated muscle with equal contractile force failed almost identically. That argues the main effect was loss of contractile capability, not a separate hidden fatigue factor.

Study Notes

This paper is strongest as a mechanism study for why fatigue may raise strain-injury risk.

Force changed modestly
Force to failure in fatigued muscle stayed within 93.0% to 97.4% of control, so the paper does not support a large collapse in rupture force.
Energy changed much more
Energy absorbed before failure dropped to 69.7% to 92.0% of control, with the bigger losses seen at 50% fatigue and slower stretch rates.
Failure length stayed similar
The authors reported no significant difference in length change to failure, which makes the energy-handling interpretation the key takeaway.
