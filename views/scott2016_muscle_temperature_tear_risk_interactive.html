Scott et al. (2016) muscle temperature and tear risk

This study tested whether colder muscle tears more easily. The key result was a clear threshold effect: below about 32°C, less impact energy was required to induce non-recoverable muscle deformation than at core-temperature conditions around 37°C.

Model

Study Notes

The paper is most useful as a practical temperature threshold study.

Key takeaway
Below roughly 32°C, muscle required less impact energy to fail than at core-temperature conditions. That supports warming peripheral muscle closer to 37°C before high-velocity activity.
Elastic behavior
At low-impact loading there was no clear difference in elasticity between cold and warm muscle, but at higher energies the colder tissue responded more stiffly.
Practical implication
The study argues that warm-up should not only stretch muscle groups, but also raise peripheral muscle temperature toward core levels, especially in cold environments.

Data source: Scott et al., Bone Joint Res 2016;5:61-65. The rebound-duration panel uses the exact published means, while the failure-energy panel is an explicit threshold summary because the extracted text reports significance across temperatures without listing exact energy values.
